Project Goal

Autonomous mapping of an unknown building, one frontier decision at a time.
  • Setup: a robot is dropped at the door of a building it has never seen, with only a 2D lidar.
  • Input each step: the partial occupancy grid the robot has accumulated so far.
  • Output each step: the next frontier to drive to — the boundary between known and unknown.
  • Assumption: deployment environment shares structural distribution with training data.
  • What's broken: classical frontier scoring is memoryless — it throws away the fact that real buildings have walls that run straight, hallways that connect rooms, doors that cluster.

Novelty & Hypothesis

What's new here
  • We treat diffusion samples as imagined buildings, not just predictions. K=8 plausible completions per frontier decision.
  • We use K-sample disagreement as an upper-confidence-bound exploration signal — the variance is the feature, not noise to average out.
  • We isolate where the wins come from with a 4-baseline ablation, plus an out-of-domain control that proves the prior is doing real structural work.
Hypothesis
A learned generative prior of building layouts gives a robot a measurable early-budget head start in frontier exploration — but only when (1) the training distribution matches deployment, (2) the step budget is tight, and (3) inference is fast enough to drive.

End-to-End Pipeline I Implemented

Four stages. The middle two are the new logic beyond the PA assignments.
01
Data
HouseExpo: 35,126 residential floor plans. Per plan I generate partial views via simulated lidar and pair them with the hidden remainder. Augmentation (4 rotations + flips) yields 2.66M training pairs.
02
Model
Conditional U-Net, 4.16M params, sinusoidal time embedding. Trained with DDPM (MSE on noise) for 29 epochs on a T4 GPU. Loss converges from 0.033 to 0.0007.
03
Scoring
K=8 DDIM completions per frontier decision. Score = E[gain] + λ·Std[gain] − β·dist. Variance across K samples = uncertainty-driven exploration bonus.
04
Integration
ROS 2 node publishes to /best_frontier. Exploration manager (from PA3) drives the robot in Stage. Pre-computed waypoints used due to Docker QEMU latency on Apple Silicon.

Hardships, honest limits, and realtime

OOD kill. Warehouse-trained model on residential maps: step-4 advantage drops to −0.1pp. The prior is not a generic averaging trick.
Hardships
  • Docker QEMU on Apple Silicon too slow for live diffusion in Stage — switched to pre-computed waypoints.
  • Asymptotic catch-up: baseline meets us by step 20.
  • 2 / 30 hard failures. K-sample disagreement could flag these.
Realtime upper bound
DDIM 30 / K=8 = ~2 s on T4. DDIM 10 / K=4 = ~500 ms — exactly the realtime budget. Sampling every 3 cells (the speed a distilled model could achieve) lifts in-domain advantage to +10.0pp.

Conclusion & what's next

The hypothesis held, conditionally
  • +3.5pp early-budget advantage in-domain. Grows to +4.7pp on the hardest decile.
  • OOD kill: −0.1pp with a mismatched prior — the prior is doing real structural work.
  • Realtime upper bound: +10pp if inference were free. Distillation is the path.
What's next
  • Distillation to Jetson for sub-second inference on a real robot.
  • Richer training domains (Gibson, Matterport, BIM data) for cross-environment generalisation.
  • K-sample disagreement as an "I don't know" flag to fall back to heuristic.
  • Diffusion Forcing for maps — sequential prediction across exploration timesteps.
