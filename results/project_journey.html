The Problem
May 20

Blind Exploration is Wasteful

A robot exploring an unknown building picks frontiers (boundaries between known and unknown space) and drives to them. Standard approaches use a simple heuristic: "go to the biggest unknown patch." This ignores the structure of buildings -- walls continue, rooms have shapes, hallways connect.

Our approach: Train a conditional diffusion model that looks at what the robot has seen so far and predicts what the rest of the building looks like. Sample K=8 different predictions. Use the agreement and disagreement between them to score which frontier is most worth visiting.

The Pipeline

How Frontier Scoring Works

score(frontier) = E[info_gain] -- how much free space the model expects + λ · Std[info_gain] -- bonus for uncertain areas (explore the unknown!) - β · distance -- prefer closer frontiers

Model Architecture

Conditional U-Net + DDPM

ComponentDetail
ArchitectureConditional U-Net (encoder-decoder with skip connections)
Parameters4,162,849 (~4.2M)
Input3ch: noisy target + partial map + known mask (256x256)
Output1ch: predicted noise (256x256)
TrainingDDPM, T=1000 diffusion steps, MSE noise prediction
InferenceDDIM, 50 steps (20x faster than full DDPM)
AttentionSelf-attention at bottleneck (16x16) only

Why U-Net? Every paper doing occupancy grid prediction uses U-Net. At 4M params, convolutional inductive biases (translation equivariance, locality) outperform transformers which need 100M+ params to shine.
