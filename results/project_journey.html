The Problem
May 20

What if a robot could imagine what's behind walls?

Standard frontier exploration is blind: the robot picks the nearest unknown area and drives there. It has no idea if that area hides a big open room or a dead-end closet.

Our idea: Train a neural network to predict what the complete map looks like from partial lidar observations. Use those predictions to make smarter exploration decisions.

Key insight: The diffusion model generates multiple plausible completions. The disagreement between them tells us where the robot should explore (high uncertainty = high value).

HouseExpo: 35,126 Real Indoor Floor Plans

We use the HouseExpo dataset (Li et al., IROS 2020) as ground truth. For each floor plan, we simulate lidar scans from random robot positions to create (partial map, full map) training pairs.

Model Architecture
May 23

Conditional U-Net + DDPM

The model is a conditional U-Net (4.16M parameters) trained with DDPM (Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models). At inference, we use DDIM sampling for 20x faster generation.

ComponentDetail
ArchitectureConditional U-Net with sinusoidal time embeddings
Parameters4,162,849 (~4.2M)
Input3 channels: noisy target + partial map + known mask (256x256)
Output1 channel: predicted noise (256x256)
Diffusion stepsT=1000 (training), 50 DDIM steps (inference)
AttentionSelf-attention at bottleneck only (16x16)

Results
May 24

Evaluation: 82% Accuracy, 0.621 IoU (single scan) → 0.875 IoU (8 scans)

The Denoising Process

Watch noise transform into a predicted map in 50 DDIM steps:

Key Finding: More Coverage = Dramatically Better Predictions

The model was trained on single scans (~10% coverage) but generalizes to multi-scan input. With 8 scans (~55% coverage, realistic for a robot mid-exploration), IoU jumps to 0.875.

Low vs High Coverage: Same Model, Better Input

Two maps achieve perfect IoU (1.00) with just 5 scans of input.

Sample Diversity: 8 Different Completions

Each column is a different random sample from the same model. The model produces diverse but plausible layouts, which is essential for uncertainty-based frontier scoring.

Uncertainty Maps

Bright regions = high disagreement across samples. Uncertainty shrinks as the robot explores more. These uncertain regions are exactly where frontier exploration adds the most value.

Best vs Worst Predictions

Top 5: IoU 0.82-0.94 (simple layouts, good coverage). Bottom 5: IoU 0.25-0.39 (complex layouts, very low coverage). The model excels on simpler structures and struggles with multi-room mazes seen from a single position.

Frontier Scoring + Exploration
May 24

How Frontier Scoring Works

score(frontier) = E[info_gain] + λ · Std[info_gain] - β · distance where: E[info_gain] = average predicted free cells across K=8 completions Std[info_gain] = uncertainty bonus (explore where model disagrees) distance = cost to reach this frontier

Diffusion vs Baseline: +24.5% Information Gain

Orange = baseline choice, Green = diffusion choice. The diffusion scorer consistently picks frontiers with more actual free space behind them.
